# Doodle Theater: drawing brief (step 4)

**Deadline for this step: Thursday 22 Oct, evening.** Friday is for the video and submission.

What we need: **3 or 4 characters drawn by a real child**, photographed well, each with a name.
These become the puppets in the demo video, so they matter more than anything else.

---

## 1. Materials

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Plain **white A4 paper** (printer paper is perfect) | The software finds the white sheet first, then the ink on it. Lined, squared, coloured or newspaper backgrounds break this. |
| **Black marker or black sketch pen** (thick tip) | The outline is what the software cuts out. Pencil and thin ballpoint are too faint. |
| **Crayons, sketch pens or colour pencils** | For filling in. Bright colours look best on the TV. |
| One clean sheet **per character** | The software expects one character per photo. |

Avoid: glitter, stickers, glue, white crayon, very pale yellow outlines.

---

## 2. What to draw (tell the child this)

Say something like: *"Draw a character who's going to be in a cartoon on TV! Make them stand
up tall with their arms out, like they're saying 'Ta-da!'"*

**Must have**
- [ ] **Full body, standing, facing forward.** Head at the top, feet at the bottom.
- [ ] **Two arms sticking out to the sides**, with a gap between each arm and the body.
- [ ] **Two legs apart**, with a gap between them, from a "ta-da" or star-jump pose.
- [ ] **Thick black outline** around the whole character, then coloured in.
- [ ] **Big:** the character fills about half to two-thirds of the page.
- [ ] **A margin of at least 2 fingers** around it. Nothing touching the paper's edge.
- [ ] **Nothing else on the page:** no background, sun, house, grass, name written beside it or border.
  - Background things get cut out as part of the character.

**Fine / encouraged**
- Anything goes: monster, cat, robot, blob with a crown, themselves, a dinosaur, a
  vegetable with a face. Weird and specific beats "normal".
- Hats, crowns, wings, tails, spots, stripes, a big smile. Details are what the story
  narrator mentions ("she straightened her spiky purple crown").
- Wobbly lines are perfect. It should look like a real child drew it. **Don't tidy it up for them.**

**Please avoid**
- Arms glued to the sides, or hands in pockets: the puppet can't wave.
- Legs drawn as one block, like a dress or tail with no gap. It still works, but walks
  less well. One of these is fine for variety; not all of them.
- Side view or lying down.
- Two characters on one sheet.
- Scary things: blood, weapons, angry monsters. The stories are bedtime-gentle.

**Variety for a good video:** aim for different shapes and colours, e.g. one
tall/skinny, one round/blobby, one animal, one silly object with a face.

---

## 3. A name for each character

- [ ] **The child chooses the name.** That's part of the magic in the video.
- [ ] **Max 24 characters**, e.g. "Captain Blob", "Princess Pip", "Mr Spaghetti".
- [ ] **One line about each one**, in the child's words if possible: "She's a dragon who's scared of the dark."
  - Optional, but it makes the story better.
- Write the names on a **separate** piece of paper, **not** on the drawing.

---

## 4. Photographing each drawing

Do this for **every** drawing:

- [ ] **Daylight**, near a window, during the day. Not under a yellow bulb at night.
- [ ] Sheet **flat on a table** (a dark or wooden table is ideal; it helps the software find the paper).
- [ ] Phone held **straight above**, parallel to the paper, not at an angle.
- [ ] **No shadow** of your phone or hand on the sheet. Lean so the light comes from the side.
- [ ] **No flash** (it causes a white glare spot).
- [ ] The **whole sheet** is in the photo, filling most of the frame, with a little table visible around it.
- [ ] **No fingers** on the paper.
- [ ] In focus: tap the drawing on the phone screen before you shoot.
- [ ] Normal phone camera, no filters, no "document scan" mode (that can wash out colours).
- [ ] Take **2 photos of each**, in case one is blurry.

Under 15 MB per photo, which any normal phone photo is.
